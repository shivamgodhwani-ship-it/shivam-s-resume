NFHS-5 Gender & Health Disparities Study

Shyam Lal College, University of Delhi  ·  2025–26

35 years of government data  ·  structured academic report

The Question

The National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5) is one of India's largest government health datasets. This study used it to examine a specific, under-discussed metric: ailment-free life expectancy — not just how long people live, but how long they live without chronic illness — and how that varies by gender and by rural versus urban geography across Indian states.

Approach

01
Secondary Data Analysis

Worked directly with NFHS-5 government survey data rather than secondary commentary on it, to keep the analysis grounded in primary figures.

02
Descriptive Statistics

Built out state-level comparisons of ailment-free life expectancy, disaggregated by gender and by rural/urban classification.

03
Data Visualisation

Translated the comparisons into visual form to make cross-state and cross-gender disparities legible at a glance, rather than buried in tables.

Outcome

The analysis was compiled into a structured academic report for Shyam Lal College, University of Delhi — surfacing where gender and rural-urban gaps in health outcomes are widest across Indian states, as a foundation for further policy-relevant research.

Key Takeaway

Life expectancy alone hides as much as it reveals. Looking at ailment-free years instead exposes disparities that a simple longevity number smooths over — and those disparities split clearly along both gender and geography.

🧠 Venture Concept

PsychTank — MindLink

Inter-University Competition  ·  Solo Project  ·  2025

Independent Workstream  ·  Psychology-Driven Platform Concept

The Context

PsychTank was an inter-university competition where participants were tasked with conceptualising a venture that addresses a real societal problem. I worked independently — no team, no safety net — and managed the full workstream from research to presentation under competition pressure.

The Problem

Student mental health is a growing crisis — but most existing solutions fail because they're either too expensive, too clinical, or too removed from how students actually communicate and seek help. The result: students suffering in silence, or finding low-quality support that doesn't resonate.

The core tension isn't access — it's relatability and trust.

The Concept: MindLink

MindLink is a psychology-driven platform concept designed specifically for students — built around how they actually behave, not how we wish they would behave.

01
AI-Based Self-Assessment

Low-friction check-in tools that feel more like a conversation than a clinical form. Normalises regular mental health awareness.

02
Peer Community Spaces

Structured, moderated spaces where students can share and support each other — reducing isolation without replacing professional help.

03
Professional Access

Direct, affordable access to therapists — with a warm handoff from community to professional rather than a cold referral.

04
Productivity Integration

Connecting mental wellness to academic performance — because students respond to tools that help them do better, not just feel better.

What Made It Different

Most mental health platforms are designed for adults and adapted for students. MindLink was designed from the ground up around student psychology — their communication patterns, trust barriers, and the specific pressures of academic life. The behavioural insight came first; the features followed from that.

Key Takeaway

Solving emotional problems requires emotional intelligence, not just functional design. The best solution here wasn't the most feature-rich one — it was the one that understood where users were psychologically and met them there.
